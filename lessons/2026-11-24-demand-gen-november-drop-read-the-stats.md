## In 30 seconds

- Google's **November 18 "Demand Gen Drop"** is a holiday roundup of six tools. Only **seasonality adjustments** is labelled newly available; the rest were announced earlier in 2026.
- The big numbers in the post (**71% more store visits, 9x return, 50% higher conversion rate**) come from **single advertisers, unspecified test designs and different definitions of "return"**, so they are not benchmarks.
- Before Black Friday, test **one** tool against a **clean control**, starting with a **product feed** if you already have a healthy Merchant Center catalog.

## What changed

- On November 18, 2026 Google published its [November Demand Gen Drop](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/demand-gen-drop-november-2026/), a holiday post covering six items: product feeds on YouTube, checkout links, Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) checkout, a creator partnerships boost, an affiliate partnerships boost, and seasonality adjustments.
- Google labels only **seasonality adjustments** as "newly available in Demand Gen". We covered how that tool works in [our seasonality lesson](lesson.html?l=2026-11-20-seasonality-adjustments-black-friday).
- Analysis from [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/google-ads-en/google-november-2026-demand-gen-drop-feeds-checkout-seasonality/) (a Tier 2/3 source, reading Google's own pages) notes that checkout links, UCP checkout and the affiliate boost were named in Google's July drop, so they are not new this month. It also reports that Google's seasonality help pages disagreed about Demand Gen support right after the post, so check the scope menu in your own account.
- Availability is uneven. Per that same analysis, checkout links are listed for 15 countries and some formats are US-only, and UCP checkout is limited to select merchants in the US, Canada and Australia. Google's post gives no rollout dates for these.
- Google supplied three case-study PDFs: [Petco](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/petco_demand_gen_story_2026.pdf), [Javvy Coffee](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/javvy_coffee_demand_gen_story_2026.pdf) and [Shopee](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/shopee_demand_gen_story_2026.pdf). These are Google-published, advertiser-reported results.

## Why it matters

Black Friday decisions get made in the next few days. A post titled "drop" with big numbers invites advertisers to switch on everything. Most of these tools need infrastructure (a clean Merchant Center feed, checkout eligibility, creator rights) that you can't fix in a week, and some may not be available in your country at all. The practical question is not "which tool is hot" but "which one is available, reversible, and testable for me before the sale."

## The lesson: how to read a vendor case study

Every number in a platform-published case study answers three questions. If you can't answer them, don't copy the number.

| Question | Why it matters | Example from this post |
|---|---|---|
| What was the comparison? | A lift versus "before" is not a lift versus a control. | Per Relevant Audience, Javvy's 50% was measured against *other concurrent Demand Gen campaigns*, not against no creator content. |
| Which part of the change caused it? | Case studies often bundle several changes. | Per the same analysis, Petco's PDF credits only the 30% Omni ROAS boost to feeds; the other figures belong to the wider Demand Gen shift. |
| What does the metric mean? | "ROI" can be defined differently from your ROAS. | Shopee's "9x" is, per that analysis, total affiliate sales divided by ads plus commission cost, which is not standard ROAS. |

Other things to check: the sample size, the date range (holiday periods flatter everything), the market, and whether the advertiser is a large brand with strong baseline demand. A well-known retailer's store-visit lift tells a small online shop very little.

> [!NOTE]
> This is not a claim that the case studies are wrong. They are real advertiser results. The point is that they are *marketing evidence from the vendor*, good for generating hypotheses and weak for forecasting your own account.

## Worked example: testing product feeds in Demand Gen for the sale

*Illustrative numbers.* A shop spends $300/day on a Demand Gen campaign with image and video assets, converting at a $30 CPA (10 sales/day).

1. **Clone, don't convert.** Create a second Demand Gen campaign with a linked Merchant Center feed, same audiences, same Target CPA, $100/day.
2. **Split fairly.** Use Google's campaign experiments if available, or run both with similar audiences for the same dates, and don't change bids mid-test.
3. **Judge on cost per sale and revenue, not clicks.** After 7 days the feed campaign spent $700 and made 21 sales ($33 CPA) at an average order value of $80. The original spent $2,100 for 70 sales ($30 CPA).
4. **Read it honestly.** 21 sales is a small sample; a $3 CPA gap is noise. Verdict: no evidence of harm and a reason to keep testing, not proof of a win.

## What others are doing

- **Google's own advertisers (vendor-run).** Petco, Javvy Coffee and Shopee, above. Treat as hypotheses.
- **Independent practitioner view.** Guaranteed PPC's video on [Demand Gen product feed ads](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RKciK2b_8B4) takes the "when and where are they actually profitable" angle rather than the announcement angle; see Watch below. Verify its claims against your own data, as with any single-account result.
- **Older Google figures** quoted in the feed case-study PDF (per Relevant Audience: 18% more clicks at similar cost from 2023 data; 11% higher conversion value from checkout links in US data, February 2025) are also Google-reported and dated.

## Where the experts disagree

**Google's position:** these tools shorten the path from video to purchase and Demand Gen can drive sales, not just awareness. The post pairs each tool with a success story.

**The sceptical position:** the figures lack test design, comparison groups and sample sizes; metrics differ across stories; and some tools are limited to select merchants or countries. Google's own help pages disagreed on seasonality scope right after the announcement (per Relevant Audience). No independent test of these specific tools appeared in the sources we found for this lesson.

**Where evidence is thin:** nearly everywhere. We found no independent controlled test of feed-driven Demand Gen versus the same campaign without a feed, nor of the checkout tools.

**Our take:** Test a product feed if your Merchant Center is already clean and you have a conversion-focused goal, because it reuses existing assets. Skip checkout, UCP and affiliate boosts for this sale unless you are already eligible and can control the comparison. This advice is wrong if you are a creator-led brand with usage rights already in place; then the creator boost deserves the first test.

## Do this today

1. Open Demand Gen campaigns and check the seasonality adjustment scope menu; see whether Demand Gen is offered in your account.
2. In Merchant Center, check feed approval status and product count (Google's help pages, per Relevant Audience, require a linked feed with Shopping ads enabled and at least four products, and up to three days for approval).
3. Pick **one** tool to test and write down the control, budget, dates and success metric before launching.
4. Re-check the definitions: is your "return" ROAS on revenue, or something else?
5. Set a review date after the sale, so the result is judged on a full week, not a spike day.
6. Link the test to your [channel controls](lesson.html?l=2026-11-03-channel-prioritization-budget-caps) so you know where the money is going.
