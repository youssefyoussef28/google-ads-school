## In 30 seconds

- Ads in Google's **AI Mode** are now live in **10 more countries** (including Canada, Australia, India and Singapore), with the **UK, Brazil and Mexico** next. Your Search, Shopping and PMax campaigns are **automatically eligible**, with **no opt-out** and **no separate reporting**.
- Ads get in two ways: **direct** (the question is commercial) and **contextual** (buying intent appears in the AI's answer). **Exact and phrase match only qualify for direct.**
- Before you widen matching, harden negatives, check Final URL Expansion, and test with a proper experiment.

## What changed

On **October 28, 2026**, Google announced that ads in **AI Mode** (the conversational, Gemini-powered search experience) are expanding beyond the US to **Australia, Canada, India, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Singapore**. **Brazil, Mexico and the United Kingdom** are slated for later in Q4 2026. In the US, Google also launched **multiturn ads**, which can appear on *follow-up* questions inside the same AI Mode conversation. ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/advertisers-in-10-more-countries-cant-opt-out-of-googles-ai-mode-ads/), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-expands-ads-in-ai-mode-to-10-new-markets-with-uk-and-brazil-next-499644))

The key facts for advertisers:

- **No new campaign needed.** Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are automatically eligible through the normal auction, with the same bidding and no extra placement charge.
- **No opt-out.** You can't exclude AI Mode or AI Overviews separately from Search, and you can't target them on their own.
- **No separate reporting.** AI Mode clicks, cost and conversions are folded into your normal campaign numbers. Search terms from these surfaces show up only in aggregate.
- **Languages are limited.** English is the only listed language for now, and Pakistan is listed for text ads only.

> [!NOTE]
> Google says AI Mode queries are about **3x longer** than classic searches, and US follow-up questions grew **40%+ per month** on average earlier this year. Your customers are typing paragraphs, not keywords.

## Why it matters

If you advertise in any of the new countries, some of your budget is about to be spent on a placement you **can't see in reports and can't switch off**. That isn't automatically bad (Google reports similar click-through rates for sponsored and organic product listings in AI Mode, based on a 15-day internal US sample), but it changes what "controlling" a Search campaign means.

Your levers move away from *where* ads show and toward *what you feed the system*: match types, negatives, assets, landing pages and conversion data.

## The lesson: two ways an ad gets into an AI answer

This is the concept to remember. Google matches ads to AI Mode in **two different ways**:

| Matching type | When it happens | Example | Who's eligible |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct query matching** | The user's question itself has clear commercial intent | "best heat pump installer in Calgary" | All match types, including **exact and phrase** |
| **Contextual matching** | Commercial intent emerges from the **AI's answer**, not the question | User asks "why is my furnace making a banging noise?" and the answer explains it may need repair | Only **broad match, AI Max, Shopping and PMax** |

So **exact and phrase match keywords only get half the ticket.** They can enter when the question is obviously commercial, but they can't join the conversations where intent *develops*, which is exactly the type of query Google says is growing fastest.

Why does it work this way? Exact and phrase match are promises about the **query text**. In contextual matching, the thing being matched isn't the query, it's the *meaning of the conversation*. Only the "meaning-based" matching systems (broad match, AI Max's search term matching, Shopping/PMax feeds) can participate.

> [!TIP]
> Think of AI Mode as the far end of a spectrum that started with broad match: the less your customer's words look like your keywords, the more your **landing page, assets and feed** become your real targeting.

## Worked example: a Canadian HVAC company

*Illustrative numbers, not real account data.*

**NorthAir Heating** runs one Search campaign in Alberta: 40 exact and phrase keywords like `[furnace repair calgary]` and `"heat pump installation"`. Monthly: **$6,000 spend, 3,000 clicks, 150 leads, $40 CPA**.

A homeowner opens AI Mode and types:

> "My furnace keeps short cycling every few minutes and the house never gets warm, is this dangerous and what should I check first?"

That question contains no keyword NorthAir bids on, and it's not clearly commercial (it's a *how do I fix this* question). The AI's answer explains possible causes, and says a dirty flame sensor or failing limit switch may need a technician. **Commercial intent just emerged from the answer.**

- With **exact/phrase only** → NorthAir is **not eligible** for this contextual slot. A competitor using broad match or AI Max can be.
- If NorthAir adds a **broad-match** version of its core terms (or enables AI Max) inside a Maximize Conversions/tCPA campaign → it can be matched contextually.

Now the trade-off math. Say NorthAir tests AI Max and gets:

| | Before | With AI Max (test) |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | $6,000 | $7,200 |
| Leads | 150 | 174 |
| CPA | $40.00 | $41.38 |
| **Incremental** | | **+24 leads for +$1,200 → $50 per extra lead** |

The blended CPA barely moved, but the **marginal CPA of the new leads is $50**. Whether that's good depends on what a lead is worth to NorthAir. If a job closes at 30% and averages $900 gross profit, each lead is worth about **$270**, so $50 incremental leads are a great deal. Always judge new reach on its **incremental** cost, not the blended average.

## What others are doing

- **Location3 (agency) ran AI Max as a controlled experiment** across five locations of a home-services franchise (Dec 2025 – Jan 2026, using Google Experiments). Conversions rose in all five markets (+97% to +720%), CPA improved in three, stayed flat in one and got slightly worse in one. Their biggest practical lesson: **turn off Final URL Expansion** for multi-location brands, or AI Max may send people to the wrong location page. ([Location3 case study](https://location3.com/blog/ai-max-franchise-conversion-growth/))
- **Independent data is more mixed than Google's.** Google cites ~14% more conversions at similar CPA for AI Max. An analysis of SMEC data found about a **13% conversion value lift but higher CPA and unpredictable ROAS**. ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-smecs-data-reveals-about-ai-max-performance/568866/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-ai-max-for-search-campaigns-deliver-meh-results-industry-tests-reveal/))
- **Google's own recommended test design:** run a proper A/B experiment if the campaign has **50+ conversions/month**; use a pre/post comparison at **20–50**. ([Google: four steps to test AI Max](https://business.google.com/us/accelerate/resources/articles/four-steps-to-test-the-impact-of-ai-max/))

> [!WARNING]
> Case studies published by agencies and Google are self-selected. Nobody publishes the test that failed. Your own experiment is the only result that counts.

## Where the experts disagree

**Google's view:** AI Mode reaches people earlier in their thinking, with longer, richer questions. Sponsored product listings get click-through rates similar to organic ones in AI Mode, and campaigns that use AI Max typically see around 14% more conversions at a similar CPA.

**The skeptics' view:** advertisers are being enrolled in a placement they **can't measure separately or switch off**. The CTR claim comes from a 15-day internal US sample covering product listings only. And independent AI Max data shows more conversion value but **higher CPA** and unpredictable ROAS ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/advertisers-in-10-more-countries-cant-opt-out-of-googles-ai-mode-ads/), [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-smecs-data-reveals-about-ai-max-performance/568866/)).

**Where the evidence is thin:** nobody outside Google can isolate AI Mode performance yet, because reporting folds it into regular Search.

**Our take:** Don't rush to switch everything to broad match or AI Max to chase AI Mode. Test it in **one** campaign with enough conversions, judge it on **incremental** CPA (not the blended average), and keep your exact-match core running while you learn.

## Do this today

> [!TRY IT]
> A 30-minute checklist for any account in an AI Mode market:

1. **Audit match types.** In each Search campaign, what share of spend is exact/phrase only? Those campaigns are locked out of contextual matching.
2. **Pick one campaign to test** broad match or AI Max, one with **20+ conversions/month** and Smart Bidding already running. Set it up as an **Experiment**, not a straight switch.
3. **Harden your negatives first.** Add a shared negative list for job-seekers, DIY/free, competitors (if you don't want them) and irrelevant services. In AI Mode, negatives are one of the few controls you keep.
4. **Check Final URL Expansion.** If you have location pages, pricing-sensitive pages or pages that shouldn't get paid traffic, add URL exclusions or turn it off.
5. **Fix your conversion signal.** Make sure the primary conversion is a real lead/sale (not a page view) so the system optimizes for value, not volume.
6. **Write a baseline note:** today's CPA, conversion rate, conversions and impression share. You'll need it to judge the test in 4–6 weeks.
