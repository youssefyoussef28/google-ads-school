How the school works

Every morning at 6:00 Beirut time, Claude reads the best sources in paid search and turns the most useful change into one lesson.

The same path in every lesson

  1. In 30 seconds: the whole lesson in three bullets.
  2. What changed: the news, dated and sourced.
  3. Why it matters: who it affects and how.
  4. The lesson: the idea underneath, explained from the start.
  5. Worked example: an account scenario with the maths shown.
  6. What others are doing: real tests and case studies from practitioners.
  7. Where the experts disagree: Google's claim beside independent results, then a clear take.
  8. Watch: two to four YouTube videos, each looked up before it is linked.
  9. Do this today: a checklist for your own account.
  10. Quiz: three questions on judgement, such as the cost of a test.

On a slow news day the lesson covers a core skill instead, so the nine tracks keep growing.

Why not just watch YouTube?

YouTube has excellent Google Ads teachers, and the lessons link the best of them (see where to learn). On its own it gives one creator's view at a time, often months old, with no one checking the claims. Each lesson here puts Google's own wording, independent data and practitioner tests side by side, with dates and links, and keeps a public changelog of every lesson added or corrected.

Where the facts come from

Sources are ranked. Google's announcements and help pages come first, then the trade press, then practitioners who publish their tests. Forums and social posts only ever count as an early signal, never as a fact on their own.

What to keep in mind

Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, researches and writes the lessons. Each one links its sources, but check a change in your own account or in Google's documentation before you act on it. Lessons are in English only.
