How it works

A school that writes itself every morning

Every day at 9:30 AM, Claude reads the best sources in paid search and turns the most useful change into a class.

Every lesson follows the same path

  1. In 30 seconds: the whole lesson in three bullets.
  2. What changed: the news, with sources.
  3. Why it matters: who it affects and how.
  4. The lesson: the underlying concept, explained from first principles.
  5. Worked example: a realistic account scenario with the maths shown.
  6. What others are doing: real tests and case studies from practitioners.
  7. Where the experts disagree: Google's claims next to independent results, plus our take.
  8. Watch: hand-picked YouTube videos that go deeper.
  9. Do this today: a checklist for your own account.
  10. Quiz: three questions that test judgment, not trivia.

On slow news days, the class covers a core skill instead, so the curriculum keeps building across nine tracks.

Why not just YouTube?

YouTube has brilliant Google Ads teachers, and we link to the best of them every day (see where to learn). But on its own, YouTube gives you one creator's opinion at a time, often months out of date, with nobody checking the claims and no path from beginner to expert. This school is the layer on top:

  • Verified: every claim about a Google change is traced back to Google itself, and every performance claim to a real test.
  • Every viewpoint in one place: Google's numbers, independent data and top practitioners' tests, side by side.
  • Current: written fresh every morning, dated, with a public changelog.
  • A curriculum, not a feed: nine tracks that build on each other.
  • Practice: worked maths, a checklist for your account, and a quiz.
  • A conversation: vote on every lesson and share your own results in the discussion.

Where the information comes from

Sources are ranked. Google's own announcements and documentation come first, then the top industry publications, then experienced practitioners who publish real tests. Forums and social posts are treated as early signals only, never as fact on their own.

Lessons are researched and written by AI. They link to their sources, but always double-check a change in your own account or in Google's documentation before acting on it.
